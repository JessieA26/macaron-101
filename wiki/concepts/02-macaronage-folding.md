# 02. Macaronage: Folding Techniques & Batter Consistency

**Macaronage** is the folding step that turns stiff meringue into smooth batter. You mix in almond flour and powdered sugar and press out some of the air from your meringue([01. Meringue Methods](01-meringue-methods.md)), until the batter flows slowly like ribbons. `[Source 3: King Arthur Baking]`, `[Source 4: Pastreez]`.

## Step-by-Step Folding Technique
1. **Add Dry Ingredients in Stages:** Sift almond flour and powdered sugar together, then fold into the meringue in 2 to 3 additions rather than all at once to avoid deflating the foam unevenly `[Source 4: Pastreez]`, `[Source 5: Sally's Baking Addiction]`.
2. **J-Fold + Bowl Smearing:** Use a flexible spatula to cut down the center, sweep around the bottom of the bowl, and gently press the batter against the side of the bowl to release excess air `[Source 3: King Arthur Baking]`.
3. **Check Frequently Near the End:** Once dry streaks disappear, check consistency every 3–5 strokes—a single extra fold can push batter from ideal to over-mixed `[Source 3: King Arthur Baking]`, `[Source 5: Sally's Baking Addiction]`.

## Ready Visual for the "Sweet Spot"
- **The Ribbon / Figure-8 Test:** Lifting the spatula lets batter flow in a thick, continuous ribbon without breaking, allowing you to draw a figure-8 `[Source 3: King Arthur Baking]`, `[Source 4: Pastreez]`.
- **The 10-to-20 Second Melt Test:** The ribbon of batter settles back into the surface of the bowl smoothly within roughly 10–20 seconds, leaving no sharp peaks `[Source 3: King Arthur Baking]`, `[Source 5: Sally's Baking Addiction]`.

## How Folding Directly Changes the Baked Shell

| Batter State | Visual Appearance in Bowl | Effect on Baked Macaron ([04. Troubleshooting](04-troubleshooting.md)) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Under-Mixed** | Thick, clumpy; breaks off spatula in chunks; stiff peaks remain `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` | Bumpy tops ("nipples"), cracked shells, uneven/lopsided rise `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` |
| **Ideal (*Macaronage* Sweet Spot)** | Glossy, flows like slow lava/honey; ribbons smoothly and melts back `[Source 3]`, `[Source 4]` | Smooth shiny tops, straight ruffled "feet," tender interior `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` |
| **Over-Mixed** | Runny, watery; pours immediately off spatula with zero ribbon resistance `[Source 3]`, `[Source 4]` | Batter spreads flat on tray, fragile/cracked tops, no feet, hollow interiors `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` |

## Conflicts & Verification Notes
- **Terminology & Timing Variation:** `[Source 4: Pastreez]` emphasizes dividing dry ingredients into two halves and counting strokes, while `[Source 3: King Arthur Baking]` warns against relying on an exact stroke count because egg white size, humidity, and meringue type ([01. Meringue Methods](01-meringue-methods.md)) change how many folds are needed. **Resolution:** Teach visual cues (ribbon + melt time) over a fixed stroke number.

# 01. Meringue Methods: French vs. Swiss vs. Italian

Why do some macarons come out smooth and perfect while others crack or collapse? It often starts with the meringue. The way sugar meets egg whites controls how sturdy the foam is, how shiny it looks, and how sesitive it is during [02. Macaronage Folding](02-macaronage-folding.md) `[Source 1: CookbookManager]`.

## Comparison Overview
| Feature | French Meringue | Swiss Meringue | Italian Meringue |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Preparation Method** | Granulated sugar whipped directly into raw egg whites `[Source 1]`, `[Source 5]` | Egg whites + sugar gently heated over a double boiler (bain-marie) until dissolved, then whipped `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` | Hot sugar syrup (boiled to ~115–118°C / 240–244°F) poured into whipping egg whites `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` |
| **Heat Applied?** | None (cold/room-temp method) `[Source 1]` | Indirect gentle heat before whipping `[Source 2]` | Direct high-heat syrup during whipping `[Source 1]` |
| **Stability** | Lowest — air bubbles deflate easily during folding `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` | Medium-High — warm sugar dissolves completely, creating a dense, glossy foam `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` | Highest — cooked egg proteins resist over-mixing and humidity `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` |
| **Shell Appearance** | Lighter, slightly more rustic matte/satin finish `[Source 2]` | Smooth, dense, and noticeably shinier shells `[Source 2]` | Very smooth, uniform, thick-walled glossy shells `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` |
| **Beginner Difficulty** | Easiest setup (no thermometer), but easiest to over-fold `[Source 5]` | Moderate (requires double boiler & temperature check) `[Source 2]` | Hardest (requires sugar thermometer & hot syrup handling) `[Source 1]` |

## How Meringue Choice Affects the Final Macaron
- **French Meringue:** Because the whites are uncooked, the protein matrix is delicate. Beginners must be extra careful during [02. Macaronage Folding](02-macaronage-folding.md) not to knock out too much air, or shells turn flat and hollow `[Source 2]`, `[Source 5]`.
- **Swiss Meringue:** Heating whites and sugar together dissolves all sugar crystals before whipping, preventing grainy shells and yielding a more forgiving batter `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]`.
- **Italian Meringue:** The hot syrup partially cooks the egg whites while whipping, producing the most stable foam that yields fuller interiors and fewer hollow shells (see [04. Troubleshooting](04-troubleshooting.md)) `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]`.

## Conflicts & Verification Notes
- **Beginner Recommendation Conflict:** `[Source 5: Sally's Baking Addiction]` recommends **French meringue** as best for beginners because it avoids hot syrup/thermometers, whereas `[Source 2: Madguru]` notes that **Swiss or Italian meringue** is technically more easier during folding because the foam is harder to deflate. **Resolution:** Keep both perspectives in the Wiki—French is *equipment-simple*, while Swiss/Italian are *structurally easier*.

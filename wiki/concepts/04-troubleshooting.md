# 04. Beginner Troubleshooting Matrix

Most macaron defects trace directly back to either meringue stability ([01. Meringue Methods](01-meringue-methods.md)), folding ([02. Macaronage Folding](02-macaronage-folding.md)), or resting/oven heat ([03. Prep, Resting & Baking](03-prep-resting-baking.md)) `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]`.

| Problem | Root Cause | Fix for Next Batch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cracked Tops** | 1. Batter under-rested (no dry skin formed)<br>2. Oven temperature too hot<br>3. Trapped air bubbles not tapped out `[Source 5]` | Rest until shells feel dry to the touch; lower oven temp by 10–15°F; tap trays firmly `[Source 5]` |
| **Hollow Shells** | 1. Over-whipped meringue (too much air, weak structure)<br>2. Over-mixed batter during macaronage<br>3. Oven too hot (shell sets before interior cooks) `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` | Stop whipping meringue at stiff—not dry/clumpy—peaks; try Swiss or Italian meringue for stronger walls `[Source 1]`, `[Source 2]` |
| **No Feet / Flat Shells** | 1. Over-mixed batter (air completely deflated)<br>2. Oily/expired almond flour<br>3. Batter skipped resting `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` | Check consistency every 3 folds ([02. Macaronage Folding](02-macaronage-folding.md)); use fresh, dry almond flour `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` |
| **Bumpy / Pointy Tops** | Under-mixed batter (still too stiff to settle flat) `[Source 3]`, `[Source 5]` | Perform 3–5 more folding strokes until ribbon melts smoothly within 10–20 seconds `[Source 3]` |
| **Lopsided / Crooked Rise** | Uneven oven heat, warped baking pan, or piping bag held at an angle `[Source 5]` | Pipe straight down at 90°, rotate pan halfway through baking, use flat heavy pans `[Source 5]` |

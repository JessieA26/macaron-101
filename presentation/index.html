Part 1 · Meringue

Three ways to make meringue

No heat

French

  • Granulated sugar whipped directly into raw egg whites
  • Cold / room-temperature method
  • Stability: lowest, bubbles deflate easily during folding
Gentle heat

Swiss

  • Egg whites + sugar gently heated over a double boiler (bain-marie) until dissolved, then whipped
  • Stability: medium-high, dense and glossy foam
Hot syrup

Italian

  • Sugar syrup boiled to ~115–118°C / 240–244°F, poured into whipping egg whites
  • Stability: highest, resists over-mixing and humidity

Part 1 · Meringue

How your meringue choice shows up in the shell

French

Uncooked whites make a delicate protein matrix. Knock out too much air during folding and shells turn flat and hollow.

Swiss

Heating dissolves all sugar crystals before whipping. That prevents grainy shells and gives a more forgiving batter.

Italian

Hot syrup partially cooks the whites while whipping. Result: the most stable foam, fuller interiors, fewer hollow shells.

Part 2 · Folding

What is macaronage?

Macaronage is the folding step that turns stiff meringue into smooth batter. You mix in almond flour and powdered sugar and press out some of the air from your meringue, until the batter flows slowly like ribbons.

Start

Stiff meringue

Mix in

Sifted almond flour + powdered sugar

Finish

Smooth batter that flows slowly like ribbons

Part 2 · Folding

The folding technique, step by step

  1. Add dry ingredients in stages. Sift almond flour and powdered sugar together, then fold into the meringue in 2 to 3 additions instead of all at once, so the foam doesn't deflate unevenly.
  2. J-fold + bowl smearing. With a flexible spatula, cut down the center, sweep around the bottom of the bowl, and gently press the batter against the side to release excess air.
  3. Check often near the end. Once dry streaks disappear, check every 3–5 strokes. One extra fold can push the batter from ideal to over-mixed.

Part 2 · Folding

How to know the batter is ready

🎀 Ribbon / figure-8 test

Lift the spatula: the batter flows in a thick, continuous ribbon without breaking, and you can draw a figure-8.

⏱️ 10–20 second melt test

The ribbon settles back into the batter smoothly within roughly 10–20 seconds, leaving no sharp peaks.

Part 2 · Folding

Under-mixed, just right, or over-mixed?

Under-mixed

In the bowl

Thick, clumpy; breaks off the spatula in chunks; stiff peaks remain.

On the tray

Bumpy tops ("nipples"), cracked shells, uneven or lopsided rise.

Ideal sweet spot

In the bowl

Glossy; flows like slow lava or honey; ribbons smoothly and melts back.

On the tray

Smooth shiny tops, straight ruffled "feet", tender interior.

Over-mixed

In the bowl

Runny, watery; pours straight off the spatula with no ribbon resistance.

On the tray

Batter spreads flat, fragile or cracked tops, no feet, hollow interiors.

Part 3 · Prep, resting & baking

Prep matters: even perfect meringue and folding can fail

Without proper drying and oven heat control, they still fail.

⚖️ Weigh in grams

Cups cause inconsistent ratios. Use a digital scale.

🥄 Sift dry ingredients

Sifting almond flour and powdered sugar removes coarse almond bits that cause lumpy shells.

🥚 Room-temperature whites

They whip to greater volume than cold whites.

Part 3 · Prep, resting & baking

Pipe, tap, rest: how "feet" appear

  1. Pipe vertically & tap the tray. Hold the piping bag at 90° to the baking sheet, then rap the tray firmly on the counter 2–3 times to pop trapped air bubbles.
  2. Rest 30–60 minutes at room temperature, until the outer surface dries into a dull, non-sticky skin.
  3. Bake and watch the feet form. The dry top skin blocks steam from escaping upward, so steam is forced out the bottom edge, lifting the shell straight up into ruffled "feet".
📖 Wiki: 03-prep-resting-baking.md
Part 3 · Prep, resting & baking

Bake low and steady

🌡️ Temperature

~140°C–160°C / 285°F–325°F

⏲️ Time

12–16 minutes, so the inside cooks without browning the delicate tops.

👆 Doneness test

Gently nudge a shell top. Wobbles on its feet: bake 1–2 minutes more. Firmly attached: done.

Note from the Wiki: humid environments extend resting time significantly, and every oven runs differently, so use an oven thermometer. Italian meringue recipes often bake slightly lower, around 140°C–150°C.

Recap

Your macaron checklist

1. Meringue

French = equipment-simple. Swiss and Italian = structurally more forgiving.

2. Folding

Add dry ingredients in 2–3 stages. Judge by the ribbon and the 10–20 second melt, not by stroke count.

3. Rest & bake

Weigh, sift, tap, rest 30–60 minutes, bake low and steady.

Most defects trace back to meringue stability, folding, or resting and oven heat. Use the troubleshooting tables when a batch goes wrong.
